# LinkedIn Comments Strategy

## The Strategic Purpose

Comments are **distribution borrowed from other people's audiences**. When you comment on a post, your comment appears in the feeds of people who engage with that post — people who likely never heard of you. A well-placed comment on a high-traffic post can outperform your own posts in profile views.

Most people treat comments as social courtesy. Treat them as a content channel.

---

## What a Good Comment Does

A comment should do **one** of:
1. **Extend** — add a dimension the post didn't cover
2. **Challenge** — respectfully, with reasoning
3. **Personalise** — "This matches exactly what I saw when..."
4. **Quantify** — add data, a number, a specific example
5. **Reframe** — "Another way to look at this is..."

A comment should **never**:
- Just agree ("Great post!")
- Summarise what the post said back to the author
- Be a thinly veiled pitch for your services
- Be longer than the original post
- Use obviously AI-generated language ("Certainly!", "In today's rapidly evolving landscape")

---

## The Anatomy of a Great Comment

**Structure:**
1. One-line hook or direct response (agree, disagree, nuance)
2. Your specific take, experience, or evidence (2-4 sentences)
3. Optional: a question that invites the author or others to respond

**Bad comment:**
> "Great insights! Communication really is key in consulting. Thanks for sharing."

**World-class comment:**
> "The part about clients not knowing what they want until they see the wrong thing — this is the most underrated challenge in software consulting. I've stopped doing requirements workshops and started doing 'assumption audits' instead. Cuts the rework by half. Curious whether you've found anything that works better than prototyping for surfacing the unknown unknowns?"

The second comment:
- Signals expertise
- Is memorable and non-replicable
- Invites a reply
- Is visible to thousands of people in that author's network

---

## Comment Targeting: Who to Comment On

**Tier 1 — Adjacent thought leaders with large followings (10k+)**
Borrowing their distribution. Comments get seen by their audience. Only comment when genuinely worth saying — low-quality comments on high-traffic posts damage credibility.

**Tier 2 — Potential clients who post about problems you solve**
Pure pipeline. Someone posts "We're struggling to scale our engineering team without killing culture" — that's a gift. Comment should demonstrate understanding of the problem, not pitch a solution. Plant a seed.

**Tier 3 — Peers in your niche**
Builds relationships, mutual amplification, and community signal. The algorithm also notices when established accounts in your space engage with you.

**Tier 4 — Your own posts**
Reply to every comment within 2 hours. Not just "Thanks!" — treat each as a mini-conversation. This extends dwell time on your post and signals to the algorithm that the content is active.

---

## Comment Timing

Comment within the **first hour** of a post being published when possible. Early comments are seen by more people as the post gains momentum. Comments left 48 hours later are rarely seen.

Turn on notifications for Tier 1 accounts so you can engage early.

---

## Daily Comment Minimum

**30 minutes daily:**
- 10 substantive comments on posts by target audience or adjacent thought leaders
- Substantive = adds a perspective, a challenge, or a data point
- Reply to every comment on your posts within 2 hours of publishing

---

## What to Watch Out For

- **Don't make it about you.** Your experience should serve the conversation, not hijack it. The moment your comment pivots to your own story and stays there, it reads as narcissistic.
- **Don't disagree to seem smart.** Contrarian comments work only with substance behind them. Empty disagreement reads as insecure.
- **Don't promote your services directly.** "I help companies with this exact problem — DM me" is spam.
- **Don't use AI-obvious language.** "Certainly!", "Great point!", "In today's rapidly evolving landscape" — these patterns are widely recognised and tank credibility instantly.
- **Don't comment on everything from the same person.** Commenting on 5 consecutive posts from one account looks obsessive and desperate.
- **Don't write long comments** that are longer than the original post — it comes across as attention-grabbing rather than contributing.
