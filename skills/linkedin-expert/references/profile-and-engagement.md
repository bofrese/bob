# LinkedIn Profile Optimisation & Engagement System

## Profile: Your Conversion Page

Profile is where people go after reading a post. If the profile is weak, you lose the conversion. Most people's profiles are CVs. High performers write them as sales pages.

---

## Profile Sections

### Headline

Not your job title. **Who you help + what outcome.**

- Bad: "Software Architect | Agile Coach | Consultant"
- Good: "I help scale-ups eliminate technical debt without freezing product development"
- Good: "Helping engineering teams ship faster without burning out the people who built the system"

One sentence. Specific audience. Specific outcome.

### Banner

Visual brand signal. Not a stock photo. Options:
- Your core positioning statement as text on a clean background
- A visual that immediately communicates your niche
- Social proof (logos of clients, publications, speaking events)

### About Section

Written in first person. Structured as:
1. **Problem** — describe the pain your audience lives with
2. **Your approach** — what you do differently
3. **Proof** — a specific result or two (numbers are better than adjectives)
4. **CTA** — one clear next step ("DM me if...", "Book a call via...")

Keep it scannable. Use short paragraphs. Avoid walls of text.

### Featured Section

Three slots, use them all:
1. Your best-performing carousel or document post
2. Your best LinkedIn Article (most authoritative piece)
3. Your website, lead magnet, or a case study page

This is the most underused section on LinkedIn. It's prime real estate — directly under your headline.

### Experience Section

Written as outcomes, not responsibilities.

- Bad: "Responsible for leading architecture decisions for a 20-person engineering team"
- Good: "Led architecture modernisation that reduced deployment time from 3 days to 2 hours, enabling the team to ship weekly instead of monthly"

If you have a consulting business, treat it like a portfolio. Each project/client engagement is a mini case study.

### Recommendations

Request specific recommendations from clients and colleagues. A good recommendation is a specific story with a specific outcome — not "great to work with." If someone offers to write one, brief them on what would be most useful to mention.

## Creator Mode

Turn on Creator Mode if you post regularly. It:
- Changes "Connect" to "Follow" (easier for strangers to follow)
- Shows your featured hashtags/topics
- Unlocks LinkedIn Live and Newsletter features
- Makes your recent posts more prominent on your profile

## The Daily Engagement System

Content is 50% of the job. Engagement is the other 50%. Most people only think about posting.

### Daily Minimum (30 minutes)

- 10 substantive comments on posts in your target audience or adjacent thought leaders
- Reply to every comment on your posts within 2 hours of posting (this is the velocity signal the algorithm uses)

### Strategic Commenting Targets

| Target | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Thought leaders in your space (large following) | Borrow their distribution |
| Potential clients posting about problems you solve | Plant seeds, build familiarity |
| Adjacent industries where your expertise is rare | Stand out as the specialist voice |
| Peers/collaborators in your niche | Mutual amplification, community signal |

### Notifications

Turn on notifications for your top 5-10 Tier 1 accounts. Comment within the first hour of their posts — early comments get the most visibility as the post gains momentum.

## Tracking What Actually Matters

Most people track likes and follower count. These are vanity metrics.

**Track instead:**
- **Impressions** — are you getting reach?
- **Profile views** — are posts driving people to investigate you?
- **Follower growth rate** — is your audience growing week-on-week?
- **Inbound DMs** — are posts triggering conversations?
- **Connection requests from target audience** — are the right people finding you?

A post with 50 likes from your exact target client is worth more than a post with 500 likes from random people.

---

## Newsletter Feature

LinkedIn Newsletter (available in Creator Mode) is underutilised. It sends an email notification to all your subscribers when you publish. Unlike posts, newsletters don't compete for feed attention — they arrive in inboxes.

Use case: monthly deep dive that complements your weekly content cadence. Each newsletter issue becomes a LinkedIn Article and an email simultaneously.

## Consistency as the Moat

The real formula:

**Consistent, specific, opinionated content → trust → inbound DMs → business.**

Most products and people fail on LinkedIn from inconsistency, not quality. The algorithm rewards sustained presence. Your audience rewards familiarity. Both compound over time.

One viral post to the wrong audience is worth less than 90 days of consistent posts to the right one.
