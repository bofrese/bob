# LinkedIn Content Formats and Links

Contents: Quick reference, Content Formats In Depth, The Link Strategy.

## Quick reference

| Format | Purpose | Frequency | Sweet Spot |
|--------|---------|-----------|------------|
| Short post (150-300w) | Frequency, algorithm training, top-of-mind | 3-5x/week | One idea, one opinion |
| Long post (500-1200w) | Authority-building, deep trust | 1-2x/week | Frameworks, counterintuitive arguments |
| Carousel / Document | Highest dwell time, highest save rate | 1-2x/month | Frameworks, step-by-step guides |
| LinkedIn Article | Search/profile asset, evergreen authority | 1-2x/month | Definitive guides - NOT a feed strategy |
| Video | Personal connection, trust acceleration | Optional | Only if genuinely differentiated on camera |
| Poll | Reach spike, audience research | Max 1x/month | Data to reference in a future post |

## Content Formats In Depth

### Short Posts (150-300 words)

**Purpose:** Frequency, algorithm training, top-of-mind presence

**Mechanics:**
- Hook in line 1 - no warmup, no "I was thinking about..."
- One idea per post. Not two. Not a listicle with 7 points.
- White space forces dwell time - one sentence per line
- End with a question or sharp close - not "what do you think?" but something that provokes a specific response

**What kills short posts:**
- Starting with "I'm excited to share..."
- Humble-bragging disguised as a lesson
- Vague takeaways ("communication is key")
- External link in the post body

### Long Posts (500-1200 words, native LinkedIn)

**Purpose:** Authority-building, deep trust, SEO within LinkedIn

**Best for:** Frameworks, processes, counterintuitive arguments, detailed stories

**Frequency:** 1-2x per week max - they require energy from the reader

**Mechanics:**
- Same hook rules - harder to execute at this length
- Use numbered or lettered structure - signals a payoff is coming
- Each section must earn the scroll
- Last paragraph is the second most-read. Make it land.

### LinkedIn Articles (built-in editor)

**Purpose:** Evergreen authority content, searchable, shareable as a URL

**Critical reality:** Articles get far less algorithmic reach than posts. Almost nobody sees them in the feed. They are **not** a feed strategy - they are a **search and profile strategy**.

**Use articles for:**
- Content you want to link from your profile
- Content you share in DMs as a trust signal
- Content Google can index (LinkedIn articles do rank)
- Pillar content to repurpose into 8-10 short posts

**Do not** expect articles to drive organic feed traffic.

### Carousels / Document Posts (PDF uploaded as document)

**Purpose:** Highest dwell time format, highest save rate, massive reach potential

**This is the highest-effort, highest-return format for consultants.**

**Mechanics:**
- Slide 1 = the hook - must be a standalone provocation
- Each slide = one idea
- 10-15 slides is the sweet spot
- Last slide = CTA (follow, DM, link in comments)
- Simple design > fancy design. Clarity converts.
- Repurpose from existing frameworks or posts

### Video

**Reality:** Native video performs well algorithmically but poorly for most consultants unless already known. High effort, high risk unless you have a genuinely differentiated style.

**Verdict:** Optional. Only use if good on camera or have a format that's differentiated.

### Polls

**Reality:** Generates reach but shallow engagement.

**Use case:** Once a month max - to understand your audience or gather data to reference in a future post.

## The Link Strategy

### The Hard Rule

**Never put external links in post body.** LinkedIn explicitly demotes these. Put links in the first comment. Write "Link in comments" at the end of the post.

### When to Link to Your Own Site

Almost never in regular posts. Your website is a conversion tool, not a discovery tool. LinkedIn is the discovery layer.

**Do link to your site when:**
- You have a specific piece of content (tool, calculator, case study, report) that LinkedIn can't host
- Running a campaign with a specific landing page
- A post explicitly invites people to learn more about your service

**Don't link to your site when:**
- Posting thought leadership (signals "I'm here to sell")
- Trying to build reach (kills algorithmic distribution)

### LinkedIn Articles vs. Your Own Blog

| Factor | LinkedIn Article | Your Blog |
|--------|-----------------|-----------|
| Initial reach | Low (feed suppressed) | Dependent on SEO/traffic |
| SEO | Medium (LinkedIn domain authority is high) | Yours to own |
| Trust signal | High (native) | Medium (requires click) |
| Longevity | Lives on your profile | Yours to control |
| Email capture | None | Yes |

**The smart play for consultants:**
1. Write the definitive version on your own site
2. Publish a slightly modified version as a LinkedIn Article
3. Drive traffic from posts to the LinkedIn Article (lower friction than leaving LinkedIn)
4. LinkedIn Article links to your site for the "full version" or lead magnet

**Or:** Publish exclusively on LinkedIn Articles for thought leadership content; keep your site for service pages and case studies. Don't use your blog as a LinkedIn traffic destination - it rarely converts.
